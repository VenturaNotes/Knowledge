## Source [^1]
- The energy of a body attributable to its motion. The kinetic energy is often denoted $T$. For a particle of mass $m$ with speed $v, T = \frac{1}{2}mv^2$. For a rigid body rotating about a fixed axis with angular speed $\omega$, and with moment of inertia $I$ about the fixed axis, $T = \frac{1}{2}I\omega^2$. For a rigid body in general motion, the kinetic energy is given by$$T = \frac{1}{2}m|\mathbf{v}_G|^2 + \frac{1}{2}\boldsymbol{\omega}^T \mathbf{I}_G \boldsymbol{\omega}$$where $m$ is the mass of the body, $\mathbf{v}_G$ is the velocity of the center of mass $G$, $\boldsymbol{\omega}$ is the angular velocity, and $\mathbf{I}_G$ is the inertia matrix at $G$.
- Kinetic energy has the dimensions $\text{ML}^2\text{T}^{-2}$, and the SI unit of measurement is the joule.
