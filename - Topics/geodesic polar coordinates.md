## Source [^1]
- When polar coordinates $(r, \theta)$ are used to parameterize the plane, centred on the origin, then the first fundamental form is$$ds^2 = dr^2 + r^2d\theta^2.$$More generally, at a point on a surface, there is a unique locally defined geodesic in each given tangent direction. Using geodesic polars, so that coordinates $(r, \theta)$ are assigned to the point distance $r$ along the geodesic in direction $\theta$, the first fundamental form has $E = 1, F = 0$, as above.
