## Source [^1]
- If $p$ and $q$ are statements then ‘$p$ not and $q$’, denoted by $p \uparrow q$, is true unless both $p$ and $q$ are true. Since ‘not and’ is cumbersome language, this is often referred to as ‘nand’ in logic. The truth table is as follows:

| $p$ | $q$ | $p \uparrow q$ |
|---|---|---|
| T | T | F |
| T | F | T |
| F | T | T |
| F | F | T |
