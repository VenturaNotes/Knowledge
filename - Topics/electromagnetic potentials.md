## Source [^1]
- Maxwell's equations can be simplified by defining an electric scalar potential $\varphi$ and a magnetic vector potential $\mathbf{A}$ in terms of which $\mathbf{E}$, the electric field, and $\mathbf{B}$, the magnetic field, can be expressed. $\mathbf{A}$ and $\varphi$ are given by$$ \mathbf{B} = \nabla \times \mathbf{A} \quad \text{and} \quad \mathbf{E} = -\nabla \varphi - \frac{1}{c} \frac{\partial \mathbf{A}}{\partial t} $$where $c$ is the speed of light. $\mathbf{A}$ and $\varphi$ are not uniquely determined by these expressions, and they are normally chosen to satisfy the extra condition $\nabla \cdot \mathbf{A} + \frac{1}{c} \frac{\partial \varphi}{\partial t} = 0$.
