## Source [^1]
- With every conservative force there is associated a potential energy, often denoted as $V$, defined up to an additive constant. The change in potential energy during a time interval is defined to be the negative of the work done by the force during that interval. In detail, the change in $V$ during the time interval from $t_1 \le t \le t_2$, or equally from position $\mathbf{r} = \mathbf{p}_1$ to $\mathbf{r} = \mathbf{p}_2$ is equal to$$-\int_{t_1}^{t_2} \mathbf{F} \cdot \mathbf{v} \, dt = -\int_{\mathbf{p}_1}^{\mathbf{p}_2} \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{r}$$where $\mathbf{v} = d\mathbf{r}/dt$ is the velocity of the point of application of the conservative force $\mathbf{F}$. For example, if $\mathbf{F} = -k(x-l)\mathbf{i}$, as in Hooke's law, then $V = \frac{1}{2}k(x - l)^2$, taking the potential energy to be zero when $x = l$. In problems involving gravity near the Earth's surface it may be assumed that the gravitational force is given by $\mathbf{F} = -mg\mathbf{k}$, and then, taking the potential energy to be zero when $z = 0$, $V = mgz$. See also GRAVITATIONAL POTENTIAL ENERGY.
- Potential energy has the dimensions $\text{ML}^2\text{T}^{-2}$, and the SI unit of measurement is the joule.
