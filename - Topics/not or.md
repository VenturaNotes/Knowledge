## Source [^1]
- If $p$ and $q$ are statements, then ‘$p$ not or $q$’, denoted by $p \downarrow q$, is true only when both $p$ and $q$ are false. Since ‘not or’ is cumbersome language, this is often referred to as ‘nor’ in logic. The truth table is as follows:

| $p$ | $q$ | $p \downarrow q$ |
|---|---|---|
| T | T | F |
| T | F | F |
| F | T | F |
| F | F | T |
