## Source [^1]
- If the function $f$ is differentiable on an interval, its derivative $f'$ is defined. If $f'$ is also differentiable, then the derivative of this, denoted by $f''$, is the second derivative function of $f$.
- Similarly, if $f''$ is differentiable, then $f'''(x)$ or $d^3 f / dx^3$, the third derivative of $f$ at $x$, can be formed, and so on. The $n$th derivative of $f$ at $x$ is denoted by $f^{(n)}(x)$ or $d^n f / dx^n$. The $n$th derivatives, for $n \geq 2$, are called the higher derivatives of $f$. When $y = f(x)$, the higher derivatives may be denoted by $d^2 y / dx^2, \dots, d^n y / dx^n$ or $y'', y''', \dots, y^{(n)}$.
