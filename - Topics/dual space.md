## Source [^1]
- (algebraic) Given a vector space V over a field F, the (algebraic) dual space $V^*$ is the vector space of linear functionals $\varphi$: $V \to F$ with addition and scalar multiplication defined pointwise. $$\begin{align}(\varphi_1 + \varphi_2)(v) &= \varphi_1(v) + \varphi_2(v) \\ (c\varphi)(v) &= c(\varphi(v)).\end{align}$$If V is finite-dimensional, then $V^*$ has the same dimension (see dual basis).
---
- (analytic) A linear functional $\varphi$ on a normed vector space $V$ is bounded if there exists $M$ such that $|\varphi(v)| \le M\|v\|$ for all $v \in V$. The analytic dual space $V'$ is the space of all bounded linear functionals on $V$ with pointwise addition and scalar multiplication. $V'$ is a Banach space, whether or not $V$ is complete; the norm on $V'$ is $\|\varphi\| = \sup\{|\varphi(v)| : \|v\|=1\}$.
