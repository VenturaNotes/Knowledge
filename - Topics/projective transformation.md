## Source[^2]
- Real $n$-dimensional projective space can be defined as $(\mathbb{R}^{n+1} \setminus \{0\})/\mathbb{R}^*$ (see HOMOGENEOUS COORDINATES). So an invertible linear map $T: \mathbb{R}^{n+1} \to \mathbb{R}^{n+1}$ induces a projective transformation $[T]([v]) = [Tv]$, where $[v]$ denotes the projective point represented by $v \in \mathbb{R}^{n+1}$. The projective transformations form a group $\text{PGL}(n+1, \mathbb{R})$ under composition. The group $\text{PGL}(2, \mathbb{C})$ is the group of Möbius transformations, acting on the Riemann sphere.
