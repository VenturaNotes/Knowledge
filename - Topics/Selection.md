## Source[^3]
- The number of selections of $n$ objects taken $r$ at a time (that is, the number of ways of selecting $r$ objects out of $n$) is denoted by ${}^n C_r$ and is equal to$$\frac{n!}{r!(n-r)!}.$$(See also BINOMIAL COEFFICIENT.) For example, from four objects $A, B, C,$ and $D,$ there are six ways of selecting two: $AB, AC, AD, BC, BD, CD$. The property that$${}^{n+1} C_r = {}^n C_{r-1} + {}^n C_r$$can be directly seen in Pascal’s triangle. Compare PERMUTATION.
