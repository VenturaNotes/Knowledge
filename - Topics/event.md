## Source [^1]
- A (measurable) subset of the sample space relating to an experiment. For example, suppose that the sample space for an experiment in which a coin is tossed three times is $\Omega = \{ \text{HHH, HHT, HTH, HTT, THH, THT, TTH, TTT} \}$, and let $A = \{ \text{HHH, HHT, HTH, THH} \}$. Then $A$ is the event in which at least two 'heads' are obtained. If, when the experiment is performed, the outcome is one that belongs to $A$, then $A$ is said to have occurred. The intersection $A \cap B$ of two events is the event that can be described by saying that 'both $A$ and $B$ occur'. The union $A \cup B$ of two events is the event that 'either $A$ or $B$ occurs'. The complement $A'$ of $A$ is the event that '$A$ does not occur'. The probability $\text{Pr}(A)$ of an event $A$ is often of interest. The following laws hold:
	- (i) $\text{Pr}(A \cup B) = \text{Pr}(A) + \text{Pr}(B) - \text{Pr}(A \cap B)$.
	- (ii) When $A$ and $B$ are mutually exclusive events, $\text{Pr}(A \cup B) = \text{Pr}(A) + \text{Pr}(B)$.
	- (iii) When $A$ and $B$ are independent events, $\text{Pr}(A \cap B) = \text{Pr}(A) \text{Pr}(B)$.
	- (iv) $\text{Pr}(A') = 1 - \text{Pr}(A)$.
