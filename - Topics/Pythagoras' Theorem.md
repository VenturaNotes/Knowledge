## Source [^1]
- In a right-angled triangle, the square on the hypotenuse is equal to the sum of the squares on the other two sides.
- Thus, if the hypotenuse, the side opposite the right angle, has length $c$ and the other two sides have lengths $a$ and $b$, then $a^2 + b^2 = c^2$. One elegant proof is obtained by dividing up a square of side $a + b$ in two different ways as shown in the figure, and equating areas.
