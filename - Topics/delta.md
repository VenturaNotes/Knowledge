## Source [^1]
- The Greek letter d, written $\delta$, capital $\Delta$. A small increment in the value of the variable $x$ is usually denoted by $\delta x$. When $y$ is a function of $x$, $\frac{\delta y}{\delta x}$ represents the average rate of change of $y$ with respect to $x$ at a point and the derivative of $y$ with respect to $x$ is defined as the limit of that ratio as $\delta x \rightarrow 0$. So$$y' = \frac{dy}{dx} = \lim_{\delta x \rightarrow 0} \frac{\delta y}{\delta x}.$$
