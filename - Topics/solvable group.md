## Source [^1]
- A group $G$ is solvable (or soluble) if there exists a sequence of subgroups$$\{e\}=G_0\lhd G_1\lhd \dots \lhd G_{n-1}\lhd G_n=G$$such that the quotient groups $G_{i+1}/G_i$ are abelian groups. For a finite group, this is equivalent to the group’s composition factors being cyclic of prime order. It is significant to Galois theory that $S_5$ is not solvable. See GALOIS GROUP, SOLVABLE BY RADICALS.
