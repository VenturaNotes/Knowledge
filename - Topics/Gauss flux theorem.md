## Source [^1]
- If a closed surface $R$ contains matter of total mass $M$ and if $\mathbf{F}$ denotes the gravitational field, then the gravitational flux equals$$\iint_R \mathbf{F} \cdot \mathbf{n} \, dS = -4\pi GM,$$where $G$ is the gravitational constant, $S$ denotes surface area and $\mathbf{n}$ is the outward-pointing unit normal. An equivalent differential version reads$$\text{div} \mathbf{F} = -4\pi G\rho$$where $\rho$ denotes density. There are similar theorems relating electric and magnetic fields, the differential forms of which appear as two of Maxwell’s equations.
