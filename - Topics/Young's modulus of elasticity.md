## Source [^1]
- The modulus of elasticity of elastic strings or springs made of the same material, but of different cross-sectional area, will vary, and Young’s modulus of elasticity states the relationship as $E = \frac{\lambda}{A}$ giving Hooke's law as $T = \frac{EA}{l}x$ where $E =$ Young’s modulus of elasticity, $\lambda =$ modulus of elasticity, $A =$ cross-sectional area, $l =$ natural length, and $x =$ extension from natural length.
