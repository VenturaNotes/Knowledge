## Source [^1]
- Now proven, the conjecture relates to optimal ways to pack spheres in three-dimensional space. The conjecture claims that the best ways are cubic close packing or hexagonal close packing, as shown in the figures. Each has a density of $\pi / (3\sqrt{2})$, which is approximately 74%.

- Thomas Hales gave a computer-assisted proof of the conjecture in 1998; at the time the proof consisted of 250 pages of mathematics and 3 gigabytes of data. By comparison, the optimal circle-packing arrangement in the plane was shown to be hexagonal packing in 1890 by Axel Thue; its density is $\pi / (\sqrt{12})$, which is approximately 90.7%.
