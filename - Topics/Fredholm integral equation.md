## Source [^1]
- The integral equations$$\varphi(x) = \int_{a}^{b} K(x, t) f(t) \text{ dt}$$and$$f(x) = \int_{a}^{b} K(x, t) f(t) \text{ dt} + \varphi(x),$$are respectively known as Fredholm equations of the first and second kind. Here $\varphi$ and $K$ are given and a solution $f$ is sought.
- Equations of the first kind often have no solution, or if a solution exists, it may not be unique or depend continuously on $\varphi(x)$. Equations of the second kind can be shown to have a unique solution when $K$ a continuous function with a suitably small norm.
