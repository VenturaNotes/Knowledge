### Of a Graph
- An edge joins two vertices of a graph. If another vertex is introduced onto that edge, it creates two edges, so in the figures below, the insertion of the vertex $C$ has subdivided the edge $AB$ into two edges, namely $AC$ and $CB$. The extra vertex $C$ is necessarily of degree $2$. A subdivision of a graph is a graph modified by the addition of one or more vertices of degree $2$ into an existing edge or edges.

### Of a Surface
- (of a surface) A subdivision of a closed surface $X$ is a finite collection of vertices and edges in $X$ such that (i) every edge begins and ends in a vertex, (ii) edges intersect in vertices, (iii) the complement of the vertices and edges has connected components which are homeomorphic to an open disc; these are known as faces. The Euler characteristic of $X$ the surface then equals $V-E+F$, where $V, E, F$ denotes the number of vertices, edges, and faces respectively. See TRIANGULATION.
