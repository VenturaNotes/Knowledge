## Source[^2]
- Given a point $P$ and a non-degenerate conic $C$, a line $L$ can be associated with $P$ as follows. Two tangent lines to $C$ pass through $P$, and the points of tangency define the line $L$. This line is known as the polar of $P$, and $P$ is known as the pole of $L$.
- ![[Pasted image 20260912104255.png|323]]
	- A pole $P$ and polar $L$
- In a projective plane (see PROJECTIVE SPACE), this process gives a one-to-one correspondence between points and lines. If the conic has equation $\mathbf{x}^{\text{T}}\mathbf{Bx} = 0$, where $\mathbf{B}$ is a $3 \times 3$ symmetric invertible matrix, and $P$ has coordinate vector $\mathbf{p}$, then the equation of the polar is $\mathbf{x}^{\text{T}}\mathbf{Bp} = 0$.
