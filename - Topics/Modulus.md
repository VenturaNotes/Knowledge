## Source [^1]
- (moduli) If $z$ is a complex number and $z = x + yi$, the modulus of $z$, denoted by $|z|$ (read as ‘mod $z$’), is equal to $\sqrt{x^2 + y^2}$
- Modulus is multiplicative, which means $|zw| = |z||w|$ for complex numbers $z, w$, and also satisfies the triangle inequality $|z + w| \le |z| + |w|$. If $z$ is represented by the point $P$ in the complex plane, the modulus of $z$ equals the distance $|OP|$. Thus $|z| = r$, where $(r, \theta)$ are the polar coordinates of $P$. If $z$ is real, the modulus of $z$ equals the absolute value of the real number, so the two uses of the same notation are consistent, and the term ‘modulus’ may be used for ‘absolute value’.
- The real modulus function $|x|$ is an example of a function which is not differentiable. Its left derivative at 0 is $-1$, but its right derivative is 1.
