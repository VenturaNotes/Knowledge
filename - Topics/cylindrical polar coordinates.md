## Source [^1]
- A technique for giving three-dimensional coordinates $(r,\phi ,z)$ of a point. Radial distance $r$ is the distance of the point from the vertical $z$-axis, azimuth angle $\phi$ specifies a rotation from the $x$-axis, and height $z$ gives the distance to move parallel to the $z$-axis to reach the point. See also POLAR COORDINATES; SPHERICAL POLAR COORDINATES.

## Source[^2]
- A coordinate system for 3-dimensional space which is a useful alternative to Cartesian coordinates when describing a problem with a line of symmetry. With the $z$-axis as that line of symmetry, cylindrical polar coordinates are related to Cartesian coordinates by$$x = \rho \cos \phi, \quad y = \rho \sin \phi, \quad z = z.$$

- Conversely, the cylindrical polar coordinates can be found from $(x, y, z)$ by:$$\rho = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2}, \quad \cos \phi = \frac{x}{\sqrt{x^2 + y^2}}, \quad \sin \phi = \frac{y}{\sqrt{x^2 + y^2}}, \quad z = z$$
