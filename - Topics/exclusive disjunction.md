## Source [^1]
- If $p$ and $q$ are statements, then the statement 'p or q' where exclusive disjunction is intended, often written as 'p $\times$ or q' or $p \underline{\vee} q$, is true only if exactly one of $p, q$ is true. The truth table is therefore as follows:

| $p$ | $q$ | $p \underline{\vee} q$ |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| T | T | F |
| T | F | T |
| F | T | T |
| F | F | F |

- The set-theoretic equivalent of exclusive disjunction is symmetric difference. See also INCLUSIVE DISJUNCTION.
