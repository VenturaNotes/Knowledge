## Source [^1]
- A subset $\mathcal{A}$ of a metric space $M$ is open if every point of $\mathcal{A}$ is an interior point of $\mathcal{A}$ or, equivalently, around each point of $\mathcal{A}$ there exists an open ball of $M$ which is contained in $\mathcal{A}$. The open sets form the topology of $M$. In a topological space the open sets are, by definition, those sets in the topology.
- Note that openness is a relative term and that open sets are open *in* another set. So $[0, 1)$ is not open in $\mathbb{R}$ as 0 is not an interior point but $[0, 1)$ is open in $[0, \infty)$. See CLOPEN SET, CLOSED SET.
