## Source [^1]
- A quadrilateral in which (i) both pairs of opposite sides are parallel and (ii) the lengths of opposite sides are equal. Either property, (i) or (ii), in fact implies the other. The area of a parallelogram equals ‘base times height’. That is to say, if one pair of parallel sides, of length $b$, are a distance $h$ apart, the area equals $bh$. Alternatively, if the other pair of sides have length $a$ and $\theta$ is the angle between adjacent sides, the area equals $ab \sin \theta$, so the area also equals $|\mathbf{a} \times \mathbf{b}|$, the magnitude of the vector product of the sides as vectors.
