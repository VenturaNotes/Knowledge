## Source [^1]
- The Hessian of a differentiable function $f(x,y)$ of two variables is the matrix$$ H = \begin{pmatrix} f_{xx} & f_{xy} \\ f_{yx} & f_{yy} \end{pmatrix}. $$
- If the partial derivatives are continuous functions, then the mixed derivatives are equal and so $H$ is symmetric. The Hessian appears in the Taylor series of $f$ as$$\begin{align}&f(x + h, y + k)\\&= f(x,y) + (hf_x + kf_y) + \frac{1}{2}(h^2 f_{xx} + 2hkf_{xy} + k^2 f_{yy}) + \dots \\& = f(x,y) + (hf_x + kf_y) + \frac{1}{2}(h,k)H \begin{pmatrix} h \\ k \end{pmatrix} + \dots \end{align}$$and is used in the classification of stationary points which are non-degenerate; that is $\det H \neq 0$.
