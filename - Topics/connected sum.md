## Source [^1]
- In topology, a means of combining surfaces or, more generally, manifolds of the same dimension. Given two closed surfaces $S_1$ and $S_2$, each with a triangulation, their connected sum $S_1\#S_2$ is formed by removing a triangle from each and gluing the surfaces along the missing triangles’ boundaries (as an identification space). The Euler characteristic of $S_1\#S_2$ satisfies$$\chi(S_1 \# S_2) = \chi(S_1) + \chi(S_2) - 2.$$
- For example, the connected sum of two tori is a torus with two holes, as in the figure.

- If the two manifolds are oriented, then their connected sum is unique up to homeomorphism. In a similar manner, the knot sum of two knots can be made. If both knots are oriented, then their knot sum is well defined up to isotopy. See CLASSIFICATION THEOREM FOR SURFACES, PRIME KNOT.
