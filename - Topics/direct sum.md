## Source [^1]
- A vector space $V$ is a direct sum of subspaces $X_1, X_2, \dots, X_k$, written $V = X_1 \oplus X_2 \oplus \dots \oplus X_k$, if every $v \in V$ can be written uniquely $v = x_1 + x_2 + \dots + x_k$ where $x_i \in X_i$ for each $i$. Such a direct sum is referred to as internal. Given vector spaces $V_1, V_2, \dots, V_k$, the external direct sum $V_1 \oplus V_2 \oplus \dots \oplus V_k$ is defined on the Cartesian product $V_1 \times V_2 \times \dots \times V_k$ with componentwise addition and scalar multiplication. In a similar fashion, the direct sum of abelian groups, rings, and modules can be defined.
