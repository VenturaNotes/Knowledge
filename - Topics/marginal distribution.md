## Source [^1]
- Suppose that $X$ and $Y$ are discrete random variables with joint probability mass function $p$. Then the marginal distribution of $X$ is the distribution with probability mass function $p_1$, where$$p_X(x) = \sum_{y} p(x, y)$$
- A similar formula gives the marginal distribution of $Y$.
- Suppose that $X$ and $Y$ are continuous random variables with joint probability density function $f$. Then the marginal distribution of $X$ is the distribution with probability density function $f_X$, where$$f_X(x) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f(x, y) \, dy$$
