## Source [^1]
- Let $I_G$ be the moment of inertia of a rigid body about an axis through $G$, the centre of mass of the rigid body. Then the moment of inertia of the body about some other axis parallel to the first axis equals $I_G + md^2$, where $m$ is the mass of the rigid body and $d$ is the distance between the two parallel axes.
- Rephrasing the theorem in terms of the inertia matrix, for a point $P$ at (column) position vector $\mathbf{r}$ from $G$, then$$ \mathbf{I}_P = \mathbf{I}_G + m(|\mathbf{r}|^2 \mathbf{I}_3 - \mathbf{rr}^\mathrm{T}) $$where $\mathbf{I}_3$ denotes the $3 \times 3$ identity matrix.
