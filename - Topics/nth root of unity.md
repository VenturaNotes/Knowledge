## Source [^1]
- A complex number $z$ such that $z^n = 1$. The $n$ distinct $n$th roots of unity are $e^{i2k\pi/n}$ ($k = 0, 1, \dots, n-1$), or$$\cos \frac{2k\pi}{n} + i \sin \frac{2k\pi}{n} \quad (k = 0, 1, \dots, n-1).$$
- They are represented in the complex plane by points that lie on the unit circle and are vertices of a regular $n$-sided polygon. The $n$th roots of unity for $n = 5$ and $n = 6$ are shown in the figure. The $n$th roots of unity always include the real number 1 and also include the real number $-1$ if $n$ is even. The non-real $n$th roots of unity form pairs of conjugates.
