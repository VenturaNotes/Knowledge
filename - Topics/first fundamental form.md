## Source [^1]
- For a surface $X$ in $\mathbb{R}^3$ with $p \in X$, the first fundamental form is the restriction of the quadratic form $\mathbf{v} \mapsto |\mathbf{v}|^2$ to the tangent space at $p$. If $\mathbf{r}(u, v)$ is a parametrization of $X$, so that $\mathbf{r}_u = \partial \mathbf{r} / \partial u$ and $\mathbf{r}_v = \partial \mathbf{r} / \partial v$ is a basis for the tangent space, then the form is given by $a\mathbf{r}_u + b\mathbf{r}_v \mapsto Ea^2 + 2Fab + Gb^2$, where $E = \mathbf{r}_u \cdot \mathbf{r}_u, F = \mathbf{r}_u \cdot \mathbf{r}_v, G = \mathbf{r}_v \cdot \mathbf{r}_v$. Any intrinsic (i.e. metric) property of the surface, such as area, length of curves, or Gaussian curvature, can be expressed in terms of $E, F, G$. See RIEMANNIAN MANIFOLD, SECOND FUNDAMENTAL FORM, THEOREMA EGREGIUM.
