## Source [^1]
- If $R$ is an integral domain, then a field of fractions $F$ can be defined which naturally contains $R$. The field $F$ is the set of equivalence classes of $(r_1, r_2)$, where $r_1, r_2 \in R$ and $r_2 \neq 0$ under the relation $(r_1, r_2) \sim (s_1, s_2)$ if $r_1 s_2 = r_2 s_1$. The equivalence class of $(r_1, r_2)$ can be identified with $r_1/r_2$, so that $(r, 1)$ can be identified with $r \in R$. The field of fractions of $\mathbb{Z}$ is $\mathbb{Q}$ with $(4, 8)$ and $(-2, -4)$ both identified with $1/2$.
