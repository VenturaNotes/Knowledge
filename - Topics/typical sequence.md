## Source [^1]
- (typical set) For an iid sequence $(x_1, x_2, \dots, x_n) \in \{0,1\}^n$ one would expect for a typical sequence that$$P((X_1, X_2, \dots, X_n) = (x_1, x_2, \dots, x_n)) \approx 2^{-nH(X)}$$where $H(X)$ denotes the Shannon entropy of the distribution. For $\epsilon > 0$, the typical set $T_n^\epsilon$ is the set of sequences $(x_1, x_2, \dots, x_n)$ such that$$H(X) - \epsilon \le -\frac{1}{n} \log_2 p(x_1, x_2, \dots, x_n) \le H(X) + \epsilon.$$
- It is then probable that a sequence will arise from the typical set despite, in most cases, $T_n^\epsilon$ being much smaller than $2^n$ in size.
