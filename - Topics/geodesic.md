## Source [^1]
- A curve on a surface, joining two given points, that is the shortest curve between the two points. On a sphere, a geodesic is an arc of a great circle through the two given points.
- For a curve $\gamma(s)$, parameterized by arc length $s$, this is equivalent to the acceleration vector $\ddot{\gamma}(s)$ being normal to the surface. A curve $\gamma(s) = \mathbf{r}(u(s), v(s))$ in a parameterized surface $\mathbf{r}(u, v)$ is a geodesic if it satisfies the equations$$\begin{align}&\frac{d}{ds}(E\dot{u} + F\dot{v}) = \frac{1}{2}(E_u\dot{u}^2 + 2F_u\dot{u}\dot{v} + G_u\dot{v}^2),\\&\frac{d}{ds}(F\dot{u} + G\dot{v}) = \frac{1}{2}(E_v\dot{u}^2 + 2F_v\dot{u}\dot{v} + G_v\dot{v}^2),\end{align}$$where $E, 2F, G$ are the coefficients of the first fundamental form.
