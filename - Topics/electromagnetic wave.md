## Source[^2]
- In a vacuum, an electromagnetic wave travels at the speed of light $c$. A monochromatic electromagnetic plane wave with angular frequency $\omega$ has electric field$$ \mathbf{E} = \mathbf{a} \cos\left(\omega\left(t - \frac{\mathbf{r} \cdot \mathbf{e}}{c}\right)\right) + \mathbf{b} \cos\left(\omega\left(t - \frac{\mathbf{r} \cdot \mathbf{e}}{c}\right)\right).$$Here $\mathbf{e}$ is a unit vector in the direction of the wave's travel and $\mathbf{r} = (x, y, z)$. Further, $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{b}$ are perpendicular to $\mathbf{e}$. The magnetic field is given by $\mathbf{B} = \mathbf{e} \times \mathbf{E}/c$. Then $\mathbf{E}$ and $\mathbf{B}$ satisfy Maxwell's equations in a vacuum and also satisfy the wave equation.
