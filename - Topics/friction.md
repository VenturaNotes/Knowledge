## Source [^1]
- (frictional force) For two bodies in contact, say the frictional force and the normal reaction have magnitudes $F$ and $N$ respectively (see CONTACT FORCE). The coefficient of static friction $\mu_s$ is the ratio $F/N$ in the limiting case when the bodies are just about to move relative to each other. Thus if the bodies are at rest relative to each other, $F \le \mu_s N$. The coefficient of kinetic friction $\mu_k$ is the ratio $F/N$ when the bodies are sliding, in contact with each other. These coefficients of friction depend on the materials of which the bodies are made. Normally, $\mu_k$ is somewhat less than $\mu_s$. See also ANGLE OF FRICTION.
