## Source [^1]
- A function $f : I \to \mathbb{R}$, defined on an interval I, is uniformly continuous if, for every $\epsilon > 0$, there exists a single $\delta > 0$ such that, for all $x, y \in I$ satisfying $|y - x| < \delta$, then $|f(y) - f(x)| < \epsilon$. For continuity only, the value of $\delta$ typically depends on $x$ as well as $\epsilon$. The notion extends readily to metric spaces, and continuous functions on a compact space are uniformly continuous. Absolutely continuous functions are uniformly continuous, but the converse does not hold (see CANTOR DISTRIBUTION).
