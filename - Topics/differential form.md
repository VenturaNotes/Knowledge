## Source [^1]
- A differential form of degree 0 on $\mathbb{R}^3$, or a 0-form, is a differentiable real function $f(x, y, z)$. A 1-form is written$$f_1 \text{d}x + f_2 \text{d}y + f_3 \text{d}z$$where $f_1, f_2, f_3$ are differentiable real functions of $x, y, z$. The exterior derivative $\text{d}$ maps 0-forms to 1-forms via the chain rule$$\text{d}f = \frac{\partial f}{\partial x}\text{d}x + \frac{\partial f}{\partial y}\text{d}y + \frac{\partial f}{\partial z}\text{d}z.$$
- Note how the terms here are the same as those that appear in the gradient of $f$. More generally, the exterior derivative takes $k$-forms to $(k + 1)$-forms, but the product used is the exterior product, so that, for example, $\text{d}x \text{d}y = -\text{d}y \text{d}x$ and $\text{d}x^2 = 0$. In the case of applying $\text{d}$ to a 1-form we get$$\text{d}(F_1 \text{d}x + F_2 \text{d}y + F_3 \text{d}z) = \left( \frac{\partial F_1}{\partial x} \text{d}x + \frac{\partial F_1}{\partial y} \text{d}y + \frac{\partial F_1}{\partial z} \text{d}z \right) \text{d}x + \dots$$$$= \left( \frac{\partial F_3}{\partial y} - \frac{\partial F_2}{\partial z} \right) \text{d}y \text{d}z + \left( \frac{\partial F_1}{\partial z} - \frac{\partial F_3}{\partial x} \right) \text{d}z \text{d}x + \left( \frac{\partial F_2}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial F_1}{\partial y} \right) \text{d}x \text{d}y,$$once simplified. Note how the terms here are the same as those that appear in the curl of $(F_1, F_2, F_3)$. A similar calculation shows that $\text{d}$ acts like divergence when mapping 2-forms to 3-forms.
- In general, $\text{d}^2 = \text{d} \circ \text{d} = 0$ generalizing $\text{curl}(\text{grad}) = \mathbf{0}$ and $\text{div}(\text{curl}) = 0$. Differential forms generalize to $\mathbb{R}^n$ for all $n$ and to manifolds where they can be expressed in terms of local coordinates. See STOKES' THEOREM (GENERALIZED FORM).
