## Source [^1]
- A simple form of the Fredholm alternative states that for a square matrix $\mathbf{A}$$$\begin{aligned} \text{either } \mathbf{v} &= \mathbf{Av} + \mathbf{b} \text{ has a solution } \mathbf{v} \text{ for all } \mathbf{b} \\ \text{or } \mathbf{v} &= \mathbf{Av} \text{ has a non-zero solution.} \end{aligned}$$
- The former holds when $\mathbf{A} \textendash \mathbf{I}$ is invertible and the latter when it is singular. The more general Fredholm alternative is an important result in functional analysis, where $\mathbb{R}^n$ is replaced by an infinite-dimensional Hilbert space and $\mathbf{A}$ is replaced by a self-adjoint compact operator.
