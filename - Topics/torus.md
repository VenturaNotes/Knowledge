### Tori
- Suppose that a circle of radius $a$ is rotated through one revolution about a line, in the plane of the circle, a distance $b$ from the centre of the circle, where $b > a$. The resulting surface or solid is called a torus, the shape of a 'doughnut'. The surface area of this torus equals $4\pi^2 ab$, and its volume equals $2\pi^2 a^2 b$. More generally, the term 'torus' applies to an orientable closed surface with $g$ holes, called the genus of the torus. See CLASSIFICATION THEOREM FOR SURFACES.
### Higher Dimensions
- As a surface, the torus is topologically $S^1 \times S^1$, where $S^1$ denotes the circle. The $n$-dimensional torus is $(S^1)^n$, and such tori are important in the theory of Lie groups. A compact, connected abelian Lie group is a torus.
- Also a compact, connected Lie group $G$ will have a maximal torus $T$. Any two maximal tori are conjugate subgroups, and every element of $G$ lies in some maximal torus.
