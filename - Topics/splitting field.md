## Source [^1]
- Given a polynomial $f(x)$ with coefficients from a field $F$, its splitting field $K$ is the smallest field in which $f(x)$ factorizes into linear factors. The splitting field is unique up to isomorphism. For example, the splitting field of $x^2+1$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ is $\mathbb{Q}[i]$ as$$x^2+1=(x-i)(x+i).$$
