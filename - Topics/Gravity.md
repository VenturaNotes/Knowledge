## Source [^1]
- Near the Earth's surface, a body experiences the gravitational force between the body and the Earth, which may be taken to be constant. The resulting acceleration due to gravity is $-g\mathbf{k}$, where $\mathbf{k}$ is a unit vector directed vertically upwards from the Earth's surface, assumed to be a horizontal plane. The constant $g$, which is the magnitude of the acceleration due to gravity, is equal to $GM/R^2$, where $G$ is the \gravitational constant, $M$ is the mass of the Earth and $R$ is the radius of the Earth. Near the Earth's surface, the value of $g$ may be taken as $9.81\text{ ms}^{-2}$, though it varies between $9.78\text{ ms}^{-2}$ at the equator and $9.83\text{ ms}^{-2}$ at one of the poles.
