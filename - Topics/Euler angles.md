## Source [^1]
- Let $\mathbf{R}$ be an orthogonal $3 \times 3$ matrix with determinant $1$; so $\mathbf{R}$ represents a general rotation of 3-dimensional space about an axis through the origin. Then there exist Euler angles $\alpha, \beta, \gamma$ in the ranges$$-180^\circ < \alpha \le 180^\circ, 0^\circ \le \beta \le 180^\circ, -180^\circ < \gamma \le 180^\circ$$such that $\mathbf{R} = \mathbf{X}(\alpha)\mathbf{Y}(\beta)\mathbf{X}(\gamma)$ where $\mathbf{X}(\theta)$ denotes rotation about the $x$-axis by $\theta$ and $\mathbf{Y}(\theta)$ denotes rotation about the $y$-axis by $\theta$. Euler angles are useful in describing rotations of rigid bodies.
