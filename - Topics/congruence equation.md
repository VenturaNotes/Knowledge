## Source [^1]
- An equation involving congruences. As division is not always well defined in modulo $n$ arithmetic, not all standard algebraic operations apply; for example, the congruence congruence $ax \equiv b \pmod{n}$ has a unique solution in $\mathbb{Z}_n$ if and only if $a$ and $n$ are coprime. Also $x^2 - 1 \equiv 0 \pmod{8}$ is a quadratic equation with four solutions $x \equiv 1, 3, 5, 7 \pmod{8}$, factorizable in two different ways: $x^2 - 1 \equiv (x - 1)(x + 1) \equiv (x - 3)(x - 5)$. When the modulus $n$ is prime, $\mathbb{Z}_n$ is a field and these issues do not arise. See also CHINESE REMAINDER THEOREM.
