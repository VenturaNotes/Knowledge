## Source [^1]
- A unit of measurement for angles; one radian corresponds to the angle whose arc length on a unit circuit is itself 1. A single radian equals approximately 57.3 degrees and full rotation corresponds to $2\pi$ radians. It is the natural unit for angles, and the one required for the simplest forms of trigonometric equations to hold.
## Source[^2]
- In elementary work, angles are measured in degrees. In more advanced work, particularly involving calculus, it is essential that angles are measured in radians. For example the differential of sine is cosine only when radians are used.
- Suppose that a circle of radius 1 with centre $O$ meets two half-lines from $O$ to $A$ and $B$. Then the size of $\angle AOB$, measured in radians, equals the length of the arc $AB$.
- ![[Pasted image 20260914111202.png|227]]
	- On a unit circle, the arc $AB$ equals $\angle AOB$ in radians.
- 1 radian is approximately $57^\circ$. More accurately, $1\text{ radian} \approx 57.296^\circ \approx 57^\circ 17' 45''$. Since the circumference of a unit circle is $2\pi$, one revolution or $360^\circ$ measures $2\pi$ radians. Consequently, $x^\circ = \pi x/180$ radians.
- The radian is the SI unit for measuring angle.
