## Source [^1]
- Three theorems due to the Norwegian mathematician Peter Sylow which provide partial converses to Lagrange's theorem. Let $G$ be a group of order $p^a m$, where $p$ is a prime which does not divide $m$ and $a$ is a positive integer. Then:
	- $1^{\text{ST}}$ THEOREM: $G$ has a subgroup of order $p^a$. Any such subgroup is called a Sylow $p$-subgroup.
	- $2^{\text{ND}}$ THEOREM: Any two Sylow $p$-subgroups are conjugate in $G$.
	- $3^{\text{RD}}$ THEOREM: If there are $n_p$ Sylow $p$-subgroups in $G$, then $n_p$ divides $m$ and $p$ divides $n_p - 1$.
- Sylow's theorems have more significance when $G$'s order is divisible by several primes, but imply nothing useful about groups whose orders are a power of a prime.
