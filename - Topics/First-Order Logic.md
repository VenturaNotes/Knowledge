## Source [^1]
- In propositional logic, sentences do not involve quantifiers; for example, De Morgan's Laws are laws of propositional logic. Also known as predicate logic, first-order logic is an extension of propositional logic which permits quantifiers over elements; for example, $(\forall x \in \mathbb{R})(\exists y \in \mathbb{R})(x + y = 0)$ states that every real number has an additive inverse. But the completeness axiom for $\mathbb{R}$, that every non-empty, bounded-above subset of $\mathbb{R}$ has a supremum, necessarily involves quantification over subsets of $\mathbb{R}$, and so is a second-order sentence.
