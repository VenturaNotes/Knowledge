## Source [^1]
- The curvature of a surface. If $u$ and $v$ are local coordinates on a smooth surface, with first and second fundamental forms $Edu^2 + 2Fdudv + Gdv^2$ and $Ldu^2 + 2Mdudv + Ndv^2$, then the Gaussian curvature is $K = (LN - M^2)/(EG - F^2)$. For a sphere of radius $r$, then $K = 1/r^2$, for a plane or cylinder $K = 0$, for the hyperbolic plane $K = -1$; more generally $K$ varies on a surface. If $U$ is a neighborhood of a point $p$ in the surface and $\mathbf{n}$ denotes the Gauss map, then $|K(p)|$ is the limit of $\text{area}(\mathbf{n}(U))/\text{area}(U)$ as the area of $U$ tends to 0. See also GAUSS-BONNET THEOREM, THEOREMA EGREGIUM.
