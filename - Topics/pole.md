## Source [^1]
- $n$. (in anatomy) the extremity of the axis of the body, an organ, or a cell.
## Source[^2]
- (1) Each of the terminals or lines to a piece of electrical apparatus, a circuit, or network between which the circuit voltages are applied or produced. See also NUMBER OF POLES. 
- (2) See MAGNETIC POLE. 
- (3) An electrode of an electrolytic cell.
## Source[^3]
- (in complex analysis) An isolated singularity in a complex-valued function at which the function's Laurent series has a finite principal part. For example,$$\frac{z^2 + 1}{z}, \quad \frac{\sin z}{(z - \pi)(z - 2)^4}$$respectively have an order 1 pole, or simple pole, at 0 and a pole of order 4 at 2. Note that the second function does not have a pole at $\pi$, as the numerator has a simple zero at $\pi$.
- If a function $f(z)$ has a pole at $a$, then $f(z) \to \infty$ as $z \to a$. See MEROMORPHIC FUNCTION.
